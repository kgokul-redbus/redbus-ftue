FTUE Design Actionables

FTUE design actionables

01

Clarity & trust signals

Primo education 1
Problem
FTUs don't understand Primo.
Potential solve
  • SOL-01
    Introduce Primo in a non-intrusive way on SRP, rather than just depending on the logo on every tuple. Explain what Primo is and show Primo benefits. Explore card extensions on 2 session to convey about primo buses.
  • SOL-02
    Reinforce it again on the seat layout and BusBuddy (ticket) pages. Explore header manifestations that explain what Primo buses are. These manifestations can appear and disappear.
  • SOL-28
    Make Primo logo in the collapsed bus-details tray tappable. Upon tap, explain the What and why.
  • SOL-29
    Reinforce Primo on bus-buddy. The idea is to make the users feel good about the choice they made by booking a Primo.
  • SOL-30
    Explore differentiated theme for Primo booking funnel.
Research source
Tracker · row 18 · SRP · High“Primo - use tuple real estate on SRP to give a teaser intro of the tag which is otherwise not understood”
Tracker · row 33 · SL · High“Primo card needs to be optimised for 5 second comprehension - construct pillars shown on carousel but user skip past due to first screen not offering sufficient context”
Study · “Primo, meaningless tags, time formats” (SRP 1/2)“Users have no clear idea of Primo, even if they have seen buses with such a sticker offline. It's difficult to think the call out on a tuple is clickable when every other persuasion tag isn't (which users are more interested to know more about but that lands them to SL).”Recommendation on the same slide: “Use the space beside it to call out 'One of the best in the route' or some qualifier to give some context.”
Price shock at payment step 2
Problem
Statutory charges and fees at payment read as unexplained additional cost.
Potential solve
  • SOL-03
    Communicate marginal additional costs from Cust. Info onwards.
  • SOL-04
    Make what the user has to pay more discoverable — right now it's on the header, which can be missed.
  • SOL-05
    Provide easier access to the price break-up on the payment page.
  • SOL-31
    Use "Price breakup" across the board.
  • SOL-32
    Improve discovery of Price breakup by labelling the button. Right now it is a + icon that can be missed.
  • SOL-33
    Provide clarity on GST and statutiry chargers not collected by redBus
  • SOL-34
    Educate users on redBus not charging convinience fee, commisons and hindden charges.
Research source
Tracker · row 48 · Payment · High“Communicate price shock on payments are GST or commission to Statutiry bodies and not commission to redBus”
Tracker · row 54 · Payment · Medium“Reword fare breakup as price breakup across payment page”
Study · Payments, key concerns“Charges which are not visible or understood are mistaken to be commission and add to Price shock (figure out ways to educate user about the govt charges).”
Study · Payment Page UX Audit (2/2)“The fare breakup has multiple buttons that lead to the same information, making it confusing. Its visual design also causes many users to miss or ignore it.”Same slide: “RTC users miss the disclaimer on Cust info and assume we add extra charge on payment page as we dont show the same on fare breakup.”
Play Store · Akshay Kulkarni, 1 Sept 2026“Ticket price varies as soon as I enter payment site”
Price shock from SRP to SL 3
Problem
Users see the lowest price on SRP, but the lowest-fare seat isn't visible within the page fold on SL.
Potential solve
  • SOL-06
    Improve visibility of “Onwards” pricing on the SRP tuple.
  • SOL-07
    Improve visibility of the cheapest-priced seat on SL. This can be achived by highliting the lowest priced seat on SL which is usually at the last row and not visiable in first fold.
  • SOL-35
    Show full SL on load and then transsion to bigger view.
Research source
Study · Seat Layout section note“Price shock was observed among many users as ₹500 - 700 difference was seen in SL vs SRP.”No matching tracker row — this one rests on the study alone.
Time format 7 / 8
Problem
The app uses 24-hour format largely, with some places showing 12-hour instead, and users generally prefer 12-hour.
Potential solve
  • SOL-08
    Standardise on one format across the app for consistency. SRP, Filters, Cancellation policy, etc.
  • SOL-09
    Which format to standardise on is a separate decision, informed by an experiment testing 12-hour vs 24-hour.
Research source
Tracker · row 15 · SRP · High“Explore a 12 hour time format”
Study · “Primo, meaningless tags, time formats” (SRP 1/2), point 7“Different time formats across the flow confuse users - moreover, less savvy users are more error prone with 24 hour format”
Naming inconsistencies 9
Problem
Free reschedule, free date change, and reschedule are used for the same thing in different places. Same pattern with fare breakup / price breakup, and amenities / bus features.
Potential solve
  • SOL-10
    Ensure consistant usage of terms. Cancellation policy / Booking policy, Free reschedule / free date change, etc.
Research source
Tracker · row 28 · SL · High“Content on BD tray - fix term for features as per filter (consistency), FR is called as reschedule policy and free date change on same tray, FC table (ensure clarity + deadline for cancellation is called out clearly)...”
Tracker · row 54 · Payment · Medium“Reword fare breakup as price breakup across payment page”
Study · Bus details discoverability, point 2“Multiple terms used on same screen for same property - reschedule vs free date change”
Study · Payment Page UX Audit, point 2“Users find 'Payment Details' or 'Price Details' more intuitive than 'Fare breakup'”
Study · Filters, SRP (2/2), point e“Filter has both amenities and features as themes vs Bus details in SL calls amenities as features?”
Seat representation consistency 12
Problem
Seat representation differs between the seat-layout screen and the payment screen, so users exit payment to go re-check their seat selection.
Potential solve
  • SOL-11
    Reuse the same seat component and labelling on both screens, so there's nothing to recheck.from the study
Research source
Tracker · row 55 · Payment · Medium“Payment screen seat map reference can at least have upper deck lower deck call out if the orientation is going to be horizontal (vs vertical in SL)”
Study · Payment Page UX Audit, point 4“Users have to spend effort to understand their selected seat as seat representation differs between the seat layout and exit from payment page to verify it.”Recommendation on the same slide: “Reuse the same seat component/label across both screens so the user doesn't have recheck with the seat layout and exit the payment page.”
Support reassurance early in the journey 13
Problem
First-time users want reassurance that support exists before they commit to a booking, and this isn't surfaced until deep in the funnel.
Potential solve
  • SOL-12
    Place a low-intrusion support call out on SRP along with other USPs of redBus like Lowest price gauranteed etc.from the study
  • SOL-36
    Add post booking support callout as a part of onboarding.
  • SOL-37
    Make support ingress on Ticket page a lot more discoverable. It can be a ticket related action like cancel, etc.
Research source
Tracker · row 29 · SL · Medium“Explore showcasing support assurance in the prebooking funnel (highlight regional language based support)”
Study · Seat Layout Page UX Audit (2/2), point 3“First time & Regional users want reassurance that RedBus offers customer support in their preferred language while they're still evaluating buses, to make a trusted booking.”Recommendation on the same slide: “Place a persistent support cue on the critical decision making point across pages.” Note: the tracker framed this as regional-language specific; the study frames it more broadly — matching the scope decision to drop the regional restriction here.
Cancellation and reschedule policy education 17
Problem
Users can't tell at a glance what they can cancel or reschedule, until when, and how much they'd get back.
Potential solve
  • SOL-39
    Cancellation and reschedule policy communication should capture a one line summary of the policies and the table can potentially be a tap away.
  • SOL-40
    Display one line summary of cancellation and reschdule policy along with the booking summary.
Research source
Tracker · row 28 · SL · High“Content on BD tray - fix term for features as per filter (consistency), FR is called as reschedule policy and free date change on same tray, FC table (ensure clarity + deadline for cancellation is called out clearly)...”
Study · Bus details discoverability, point 2“Multiple terms used on same screen for same property - reschedule vs free date change”
Study · Free Cancellation tableThe Free Cancellation table shows “100% refund” in green even in time slots where it doesn't apply.Paraphrased from the theme analysis — the exact slide wording wasn't in the extracted notes. Worth checking against the deck.
02

Discoverability & flow

Sort order clarity 5
Problem
Users don't see a recognisable pattern in the SRP sort order.
Potential solve
  • SOL-13
    Show “sorted by” up front, with an option to change the sort order.
  • SOL-38
    Sort orders to have sub text explaining what every sort option is optimized for. Example: Relavance (Optimized for best value), Low to high (Optimized to find the cheapest buses), Best rated first (Optimized for the best buses)
Research source
Tracker · row 12 · SRP · High“Consolidate filter categories (remove internal definitions like features vs amenities, add redundancy for toilets across multiple relevant categories) and reword sort order to make it more clearer”
Tracker · row 11 · SRP · Medium“Work towards filter prominence, sequence of filter chips, feedback on selection (chips & list), easy clearance of filters and sort order”
Filter discovery and engagement 6
Problem
Users miss filters entirely. Filter chips shown upfront aren't always relevant for FTUs. The filter page is cumbersome to consume.
Potential solve
  • SOL-14
    Remove the ad cards and collection cards on top to give filters better visibility.
  • SOL-15
    Improve filter visual affordance to draw engagement.
  • SOL-16
    Formally introduce AI smart filter to encourage usage.
Research source
Tracker · row 12 · SRP · High“Consolidate filter categories (remove internal definitions like features vs amenities, add redundancy for toilets across multiple relevant categories) and reword sort order to make it more clearer”
Tracker · row 11 · SRP · Medium“Work towards filter prominence, sequence of filter chips, feedback on selection (chips & list), easy clearance of filters and sort order”
Tracker · row 7 · SRP · Medium“Revisit banner size on SRP - banners tend to overshadow filter discoverability”
Study · “Choices, relevance and role of filters” (SRP 1/2), point 1“Top cards ignored and filters generally missed. Too many choices with no clear logic of sequence doesn't land positively with new users - depth of supply vs relevance.”Recommendation: “Can we give more prominence to filter and sort by moving top cards as a horizontal carousel in between the SRP listings - it is a waste of first fold?”
Study · Filters (SRP 2/2), point 2“Filters need to be more user friendly: a. Show relevant options first (AC, Sleeper, Time) b. Remove deadweight (Primo, Deals in RTC flow) c. Consolidated view of applied filters for easy deselection not present today d. Auto removal expected on click / clear all...”
Search widget experience 11
Problem
Too much back-and-forth. The Today/Tomorrow button takes users straight to SRP, making them miss the Women-mode option.
Potential solve
  • SOL-17
    A well-integrated search experience optimised for speed and clarity.
  • SOL-18
    Today/Tomorrow could work like tabs rather than buttons.
Research source
Study · “Mislands, wrong search, female flow” (Homepage), point 3“Different interactions for dates in search widget confusing - today / tomorrow takes users to SRP directly while a specific date requires an additional interaction the Search CTA. Women funnel penalised. Should we keep women funnel toggle on SRP too?”No matching tracker row — the closest tracker item (row 5, a Women-toggle interaction bug) is a different, engineering-side issue and sits in the parked list.
Bus-details tray discoverability 14
Problem
Most users don't realise the tray expands to show bus details. The details are helpful but go unexplored for lack of affordance, and once opened, the tray behaves erratically (close, drop-down, drag).
Potential solve
  • SOL-19
    Make the page continuous.
  • SOL-20
    Add a chevron to indicate it expands.
  • SOL-21
    Load the tray in half-expanded state on page load and tuck it away to ensure users understand there is more information inside the tray.from the study
Research source
Tracker · row 25 · SL · High“Resolve Bus details discoverability - gesture not expected and performed by users - make it auto scroll”
Study · “Bus details discoverability hampered”, point 1“Most users didn't realise the tray expands to show Bus details. The details were very helpful but lay unexplored for lack of a better interaction. When opened the tray tends to behave erratically - close, drop down, drag.”Recommendation on the same slide: “Make the page continuous, add a Λ chevron to indicate and make the movement smoother.”
03

Monetisation vs. core task

Offer discovery 4
Problem
Users don't discover offers early.
Potential solve
  • SOL-22
    Make all offers on the homepage a lot more discoverable by doing the following:
    1. Offers section should be visible on the first fold of the common home
    2. Offers section should have clear communication about the depth of deals and the exclusivity on redBus. 
    3. Offers section should have clear bucketing of platform and payment offers. 
  • SOL-23
    Surface payment offers on the payment page by doing the following:
    1. A low intrusive ingress point revealing all offers with capability to apply. 
    2. The expanded view of the offers section should have clear bucketing of playform and payment offers. 
Research source
Study · Payments, key concerns, point 1“User expect coupons to be shown on this screen. If they hunt for coupons, the experience is broken: a. The entire txn is gone - find the bus, get a new seat, fill details and then apply coupon to book your ticket (it may not work). b. Coupons don't have better context when not applicable. c. Coupons with cashback don't highlight validity of CB (a key lever to judge value of the offer).”No tracker row speaks to homepage offer discovery specifically; this is the closest evidence available, from the payment-screen coupon findings.
Timing of app permission and review requests 10
Problem
Location permission is asked at onboarding. Notification permission and the app-review prompt appear on the homepage, for a user who hasn't seen SRP even once.
Potential solve
  • SOL-24
    Contextualise requests, dont ask plainly — location permission can stay at onboarding.
  • SOL-25
    Ask for notification permission when a user drops from a session, with context and highlighting value.
  • SOL-26
    Ask for the app review after booking completion. Definetly not on home page after app install, which is what the current production behaviour. Contextualise the ask.
  • SOL-41
    Remove log-in bottomsheet on SL. (Design not required for this)
Research source
Tracker · row 60 · App · High“Stop asking Playstore reviews after log in - allow user to at least explore SRP”
Study · Home Page UX Audit, point 2“The review prompt fires within ~10 seconds of first launch leading to abandonment or poor rating.”Recommendation on the same slide: “Trigger the prompt on a positive value moment, not a timer (e.g. after a completed first booking or after availing an offer).” The study's timing (~10 seconds after first launch) supersedes the tracker's “after login” framing.
04

Error & recovery copy

Cust. Info validation error handling 15
Problem
On the customer-info page, error messages persist after the user has already fixed the issue, and technical errors (like the sold-out-seat message) aren't rewritten in plain language, so users can't tell if anything's resolved. Validation errors aren't handled well on this page generally.
Potential solve
  • SOL-42
    Update error copies with appropriate replacements. Some examples of poor copy. 
    • "Enter your name in English" when a number is input in the name field. 
    • "Please enter valid Age" is the error message when 3 digits are entered. 
    • "Please enter valid phone number" is the error when error phone number is not entered or partially entered. There should be different copies for when not entered and when incorrectly entered. 
  • SOL-43
    State of residence and WhatsApp optin can be selected by default and have a much lower visual affordance.
  • SOL-44
    Login to view saved passenger list CTA can be contextual and have lesser visual affordance.
Research source
Tracker · row 41 · Cust Info · Medium“Error messages don't disappear till CTA is clicked - confuses users whether they have fixed the error or not (frustrated when they have solved it correctly but error message doesnt disappear)”
Tracker · row 43 · Cust Info · Low“Mobweb - when seat was sold out while user was in booking journey a vague error message was shown 'IAS response null' slide 12 FTUE”
Study · Cust Info, “Archaic error handling”, point 2“Error messages remain till CTA is clicked and user is unsure whether the issue has been solved.”Recommendation on the same slide: “Responsive error messages needed - UX has evolved, we have not.”
Study · Search Result Page UX Audit, point 2“Show a clear, user-friendly error message instead of technical ones (e.g. 'IAS response is null')”

Drop-off recovery

Resume booking flow 16
Problem
No way back into a booking abandoned at the payment step. The homepage doesn't offer to resume it.
Potential solve
  • SOL-27
    Introduce a “Continue booking” card on the homepage for payment-page drop-offs.already scheduled S4 Q2 / S1 Q3
  • SOL-45
    Make recently viewed services easily scannable on SRP. We can try a small green text with a check mark on a tuple that says "seen".
Research source
Tracker · row 1 · HP · High · FTUE + UXB“Introduce a 'Continue booking card' on HP for payment page drop offs”Status column: S4 Q2 / S1 Q3.
